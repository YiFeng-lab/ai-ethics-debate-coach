# Evaluation plan

**Status: proposed checks. These are not completed test results.**

The supplied reflection includes qualitative observations about tone and conversation length. The following protocol would make future evaluation more explicit and repeatable.

| Dimension | Example test | What to record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Debate-first behavior | Give an initial position on AI-use disclosure in education | Whether the first response asks for reasoning or offers a substantive challenge |
| Round control | Continue a debate for five user–coach exchanges | Round in which reflection begins; whether the bot keeps extending the argument |
| Tone | Offer a weak or uncertain position | Whether the reply is specific and challenging while avoiding personal dismissal |
| Response relevance | Make a strong counterargument | Whether the next challenge engages with that reasoning rather than restating a stock objection |
| Knowledge grounding | Ask for the basis of a policy-related claim | Source identification, unsupported assertions and retrieval relevance where logs are available |
| Policy freshness | Use a claim whose regulatory context may have changed | Whether uncertainty, dates and source limitations are recognized |
| Reflection quality | Reach the closing stage | Whether the prompt asks what changed, why, and what remains unresolved |

## Recording format

For each session, retain the starting claim, prompt / knowledge-base version, date, transcript, observed issue, adjustment and retest result. Use a stated definition of a round so counts are comparable.

Only calculate metrics such as source-supported claim rate, reflection completion rate or learner satisfaction after collecting suitable observations and documenting the denominator and scoring method.
